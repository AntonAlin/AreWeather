How this forecast is made

No black boxes — the summary is here, every formula and licence is on the method page.

Full method, sources & licences →
  1. Six numerical weather models are fetched per mountain from Open-Meteo: MET Norway MET Nordic (1 km, the best model for Scandinavian terrain), DMI Harmonie AROME (2 km), DWD ICON, ECMWF IFS, UKMO and NOAA GFS.
  2. A real sounding, not a lapse-rate guess. Temperature, humidity and wind on pressure levels from 1000 hPa to 500 hPa with their geopotential heights give the true vertical structure above the mountain — including inversions, where the summit is warmer than the village.
  3. Elevation downscaling. The sounding is anchored to the surface ensemble at model height, then interpolated to every 100 m band. Wind is blended from surface to free atmosphere and multiplied by a terrain exposure factor. Precipitation gets an orographic enhancement with height.
  4. Phase from wet-bulb temperature, not air temperature — the physically correct way to place a snow line, then a temperature-dependent snow ratio converts millimetres to centimetres.
  5. Machine learning, trained in your browser. 45 days of archived forecasts from every model are scored against ERA5-Land reanalysis. A ridge regression learns the residual temperature and wind bias at this point; a logistic model calibrates precipitation probability; a softmax over measured error assigns each model its weight. Hold-out validation is reported honestly in the learning log — including when the correction does not help.
  6. Ensemble probabilities come from a 30+ member ICON-EU / GFS ensemble, giving the p10–p90 bands and the chance of precipitation.

This is not an avalanche forecast. Wind-loading and new-snow numbers are meteorological indices only. For avalanche danger in the Swedish fjäll always use lavinprognoser.se, and for official warnings SMHI. Mountain weather kills people who trust a screen over their eyes.
